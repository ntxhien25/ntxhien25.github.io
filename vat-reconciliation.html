The business situation

The group comprises two companies. One company handles approximately 800 VAT input invoices and 200 VAT output invoices per month. Most VAT input invoices are printed manually from MISA meInvoice. According to the company’s discussions with MISA, meInvoice imports e-invoice data from the GDT portal.

Staff also submit printed invoices for miscellaneous expenses. The VAT return preparer reviews the printed documents individually, but the company has no established workflow to confirm that the invoices available in meInvoice and submitted by staff cover all VAT input invoices in GDT.

This creates a completeness risk during VAT return preparation: an invoice may be available in one source but absent from the documents or accounting schedule being reviewed. Reconciling GDT records against MISA schedules helps identify those gaps for follow-up.

The task

Develop a repeatable reconciliation workflow that brings GDT invoice exports and MISA VAT return schedules together, compares VAT input and VAT output invoices separately, and produces a clear list of items requiring review.

The review covers invoice completeness, invoice status, amounts excluding VAT, VAT amounts and invoice totals, with source references that allow the preparer to trace each exception.

The solution

Collect and prepare

Combine GDT records for coded, uncoded and cash-register VAT input invoices. Load VAT output invoices and the corresponding MISA schedules, retaining source references.

Match and compare

Match invoice number, issue date and seller’s or purchaser’s tax code. Check net amounts, VAT and invoice totals independently, and flag missing amounts or duplicate matching keys.

Review the exceptions

Summarise the results in charts and prepare separate input and output workbooks showing matching records, amount exceptions and records found in only one source.

The workflow can save preparation time by automating the comparison of invoice records and directing attention to exceptions. Rather than manually comparing every record across the two datasets, the preparer can review missing invoices, amount differences, customs declarations and prior-period items using a traceable reconciliation report.

It also provides a consistent basis for checking input and output VAT totals before preparing the VAT return. Supporting-document checks and decisions on input VAT deductibility remain part of the preparer’s review.

Outcome: a repeatable reconciliation process that can reduce routine comparison work and leave more time for investigating differences. The project dataset contains 839 GDT VAT input invoices and 189 VAT output invoices, providing a workload close to the monthly company context.

How the matching works

1. Preserve the source

Read invoice numbers and tax codes as text. Keep the original values and source row references.

2. Match invoice details

Use invoice number, issue date and the seller’s tax code for inputs, or purchaser’s tax code for outputs. Check duplicate keys before joining.

3. Explain differences

Compare all three amounts and retain blank net amounts as missing. Present each GDT review record above its MISA counterpart.

Scope of the public demo

The project includes four GDT Excel files and one MISA workbook, retaining the source headers, column order and worksheet layouts. Company names, addresses, tax codes, invoice numbers and series have been replaced, and amounts varied consistently across corresponding records. Additional sample records preserve the matching and review patterns of the baseline. Only the input and output schedules are populated for reconciliation; other MISA worksheets retain their template layouts. Both notebooks use the same readers and reconciliation logic. Original confidential workbooks and tax-portal access components remain private.

Review classifications document project rules. They do not independently establish document type or eligibility for an input VAT credit. Demonstration results are not presented as measured client savings.
