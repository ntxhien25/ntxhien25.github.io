The Situation

Why Tax Reporting Kept Breaking Down

This project grew out of a problem that will be familiar to anyone who has worked in a regional tax function: the group's 42 operating subsidiaries each maintained their own local accounts in different functional currencies (VND, SGD, EUR, USD) with different tax holidays, different local statutory rates, and different month-end close timelines.

The OECD BEPS Pillar Two framework added another layer — from 2024, any multinational with consolidated revenues above €750M is subject to a global 15% minimum tax per jurisdiction. That meant we needed to know, for every entity, whether its effective tax rate fell below that threshold and by how much. The existing spreadsheet process couldn't answer that question without significant manual effort — and certainly not in time to do anything about it.

The Problem

What the Spreadsheet Process Actually Cost Us

  • Month-end close took three weeks, not three days: Each local controller submitted a separate Excel workbook by email. Group Tax then spent the better part of a week converting currencies, reconciling book-to-tax differences, and chasing corrections for data quality issues that only surfaced after consolidation.
  • No visibility on Pillar 2 exposure until the year-end audit: Without a consolidated ETR view, the executive committee didn't know which overseas entities — Singapore treasury, Ireland distribution hubs — were below the 15% GloBE threshold. That's not a planning gap; it's an audit risk.
  • Scenario modeling required an external advisor: If a business unit wanted to know the tax impact of restructuring an intercompany service arrangement, that question went to an external consultant. There was no internal capability to model it quickly.
The CFO's question after one quarterly close: "Can anyone tell me if we owe top-up tax next year — and how much?" At the time, the honest answer was: not until we finish the statutory filings.
The Architecture

Data Model Design — and the Grain Decision that Matters Most

The decision I spent the most time on was the data model grain. Tax consolidation works at the entity-month level for most calculations, but Pillar 2 substance-based carve-outs require payroll and asset data that often lives at a different granularity. Getting that wrong at the design stage means your DAX measures either over-aggregate or produce BLANK errors in ways that are hard to debug.

The final star schema uses four main tables:

  • Fact_EntityFinancials — Grain: Entity × Month. Stores revenue, profit before tax (PBT), current tax accrued, deferred tax movement, eligible payroll, and asset book values in local currency.
  • Fact_TaxAdjustments — Permanent additions (non-deductible expenses: excessive entertainment, tax penalties) and temporary differences, allowing book-to-tax reconciliation per entity.
  • Dim_LegalEntity — Master corporate registry: parent ownership percentages, CbCR grouping, and tax holiday expiration dates by subsidiary.
  • Dim_Jurisdiction — Statutory CIT rates, Pillar 2 adoption status, local QDMTT laws, and whether a SAFE HARBOUR transitional election applies.

Phase 05 · Annotated DAX Library

DAX Measure Library & Logic Inspector

Browse through the production DAX calculations implementing the OECD Pillar 2 rulebook: 

// 1. Jurisdictional GloBE Effective Tax Rate (OECD Pillar 2)
[Jurisdictional GloBE ETR] = 
DIVIDE(
    [GloBE Covered Taxes USD],
    [GloBE Qualifying Income USD],
    BLANK()
)

// 2. Top-Up Tax Percentage Rate (Floored at 0%)
[Top-Up Tax Rate %] = 
VAR _JurisdictionETR = [Jurisdictional GloBE ETR]
RETURN
    IF(
        ISBLANK(_JurisdictionETR) || _JurisdictionETR >= 0.15,
        0,
        0.15 - _JurisdictionETR
    )

// 3. Net Pillar 2 Top-Up Tax Liability under Income Inclusion Rule (IIR)
[Net IIR Top-Up Tax Liability USD] = 
VAR _ExcessProfit = [Excess Profit Subject to Top-Up USD]
VAR _GrossTopUp = _ExcessProfit * [Top-Up Tax Rate %]
VAR _LocalQDMTT = [Local QDMTT Paid USD]
RETURN
    MAX(0, _GrossTopUp - _LocalQDMTT)
What Changed

Results — and What I'd Do Differently

  • 18+ hours reclaimed per month: Finance controllers now access verified group tax metrics within 48 hours of trial balance close instead of waiting until the third week. The time savings weren't the main point — the earlier visibility into compliance gaps was.
  • Proactive exposure management: The What-If parameter capability let the business model intercompany pricing restructures before implementing them. One scenario analysis identified an opportunity to avoid approximately $2.8M in unnecessary Top-Up Tax liabilities by adjusting service fee arrangements before year-end.
  • Audit-ready documentation: Every ETR figure in the dashboard traces back to the source trial balance entry. That level of lineage — from GL line to consolidated group ETR — is exactly what external auditors and tax authorities ask for under Pillar 2 and CbCR rules.

One thing I'd do differently: I built the currency conversion logic inside DAX measures. It works, but it creates a fragile dependency — if exchange rate data is delayed, some measures return BLANK unexpectedly and it's not immediately obvious why. Next time I'd handle FX conversion in the ETL layer (Power Query) so the fact table already stores USD-equivalent values, and the DAX measures stay clean and testable.
