The Situation

Receivables and collection priorities

Receivables reporting brings invoice balances, due dates and collection responsibilities together. This fictional example uses varied payment terms and partial receipts to illustrate the workflow.

Customer, staff and ageing views help a reviewer identify balances requiring follow-up. Demonstration volumes and dates do not describe a particular company.

Before the workflow

From an emailed summary to a clearer view of receivables

A basic receivables report may be an Excel summary of outstanding, due and overdue balances by customer. It can show totals while offering limited detail on ageing, changes since the previous report and responsibility for collection.

The example extends a balance summary into customer, staff and status views. Its workflow is informed by reporting experience; fictional invoice details are calibrated to the earlier aggregate presentation.

The strategic transformation

Turn balance summaries into collection priorities

A reporting framework was designed around the decisions management could make from the data: which customers account for the exposure, how long invoices have been overdue and which staff members should follow up. Invoice balances, due dates, customer terms and staff assignments were brought together in an interactive Excel dashboard.

  • Compare current and previous receivables balances.
  • Review invoice ageing and upcoming due dates.
  • Identify the staff members responsible for following up with each customer.
  • Check that customer and staff breakdowns reconcile to the invoice ledger.

The intended value is clearer management visibility, supported by filters and invoice-level detail. The demonstration does not measure business outcomes.

System architecture & user adoption

Automated preparation, a familiar Excel interface

The design separates data preparation from the management view: invoice records, credit terms and staff assignments feed the calculations, while Excel presents the KPIs, ageing analysis and collection filters. Reconciliation checks keep the dashboard tied to its supporting invoice schedules.

Excel provides a familiar interface. Users can inspect records, filter the dashboard and share the workbook through the existing email process, without learning a separate reporting interface.

Power BI or a Python web application could suit a different reporting environment. A familiar workbook keeps the calculations, native charts and review checks accessible in one file.

Reconciliation controls

Checks behind the dashboard

Source and amount checks

  • Only records with an outstanding receivables balance greater than zero are included for review.
  • Invoice gross amount less receipts applied must equal the outstanding balance.
  • Current and prior invoice schedules support the period comparison.

Coverage checks

  • Customer totals reconcile to the selected invoice balance.
  • Staff totals include every selected invoice.
  • Ageing bands reconcile to the selected invoice balance.
View the invoice amount check
=SUM('Current AR'!G6:G160)
 - SUM('Current AR'!H6:H160)
 - SUM('Current AR'!I6:I160)

A zero result confirms that gross amounts less receipts equal the outstanding balance.

The public workbook contains the dashboard, all current and prior invoice records, the calculation sheet, collection follow-up and reconciliation checks. It is a demonstration of the reporting model and contains no connection to a live ERP.

  • Current and prior invoice schedules, including every record in the demo.
  • Six reconciliation checks on the Checks sheet.

Scope of the fictional demonstration

Invoice details are fictional. Aggregate balances, customer/staff distributions and ageing proportions retain the earlier public presentation. The model preserves the reporting method and formula architecture, not source business records. The 20% threshold is illustrative, not a company policy.
