The Situation

Contractual Complexity & Trade Credit Exposure

In large industrial engineering, structural steel, and EPC contracting operations, trade credit is an essential competitive tool. However, credit terms vary widely across contracts (30, 45, 60, 70, and 75 days) and payments arrive through complex milestone draws, bank guarantees, and partial advances.

At any given point, the enterprise manages tens of billions in outstanding receivables. Managing cash flow requires continuous alignment between physical delivery milestones, commercial contract terms, and general ledger records across all counterparties.

Before the workflow

From an emailed summary to a clearer view of receivables

The previous report was an emailed Excel summary of outstanding, due and overdue balances by customer. It showed the totals, but offered limited detail on ageing, changes since the previous report and responsibility for collection.

The dashboard began as a proactive reporting improvement. The owner had not requested these analytical views; the work identified what was missing, introduced a more useful reporting structure and demonstrated how customer, staff and status filters could support collection decisions.

The strategic transformation

Turn balance summaries into collection priorities

A reporting framework was designed around the decisions management could make from the data: which customers account for the exposure, how long invoices have been overdue and which staff members should follow up. Invoice balances, due dates, customer terms and staff assignments were brought together in an interactive Excel dashboard.

  • Compare current and previous receivables balances.
  • Review invoice ageing and upcoming due dates.
  • Identify the staff members responsible for following up with each customer.
  • Check that customer and staff breakdowns reconcile to the invoice ledger.

The improvement was greater management visibility: an overall portfolio view, supported by customer and staff filters and invoice-level detail. After adoption, the owner confirmed that the report made it easier to identify whom to remind about collections.

System architecture & user adoption

Automated preparation, a familiar Excel interface

The design separates data preparation from the management view: invoice records, credit terms and staff assignments feed the calculations, while Excel presents the KPIs, ageing analysis and collection filters. Reconciliation checks keep the dashboard tied to its supporting invoice schedules.

Excel was selected because the accounting and commercial teams already used it. Staff could inspect records, filter the dashboard and share the workbook through the existing email process, without learning a separate reporting interface.

Power BI or a Python web application could suit a different reporting environment. For this team, a familiar workbook supported adoption and kept the calculations, native charts and review checks accessible in one file.

Reconciliation controls

Checks behind the dashboard

Source and amount checks

  • Only records with an outstanding receivables balance greater than zero are included for review.
  • Invoice gross amount less receipts applied must equal the outstanding balance.
  • Current and prior invoice schedules support the period comparison.

Coverage checks

  • Customer totals reconcile to the selected invoice balance.
  • Staff totals include every selected invoice.
  • Ageing bands reconcile to the selected invoice balance.
View the invoice amount check
=SUM('Current AR'!G6:G160)
 - SUM('Current AR'!H6:H160)
 - SUM('Current AR'!I6:I160)

A zero result confirms that gross amounts less receipts equal the outstanding balance.
