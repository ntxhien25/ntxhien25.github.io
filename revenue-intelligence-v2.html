01 / The business question

Can the commercial story
be trusted?

Monthly sales exports can look consistent while their column order changes. A consolidated report needs to distinguish sales from tax, invoice lines from invoices, and a phased budget from a full-year target.

The management question: where is performance ahead of plan, and which accounts or staff need closer review?

What I built in this demonstration

A transparent reporting workflow

A header-based import, one normalized sales dataset, reconciled brand and staff views, and a repeatable set of checks. The downloadable implementation regenerates this page from fictional source sheets.

Finding 01

Both invoice count and value increased.

September versus January: invoice count rose 61.9%; average invoice value rose 49.3%. The increase is not explained by invoice count alone.

Decision supported: review larger invoices and delivery timing before treating the closing-month level as recurring demand.

Finding 02

Coverage deserves a second dimension.

The brand × staff view shows where reported sales sit. Brand and staff totals reconcile to the same dataset.

Decision supported: assess backup account coverage where responsibility is concentrated. Sales allocation alone does not measure staff productivity.

Finding 03

Concentration is a review trigger.

Group 01 contributes 14.7% of synthetic sales. Customer groups are explicitly assigned in this fixture.

Decision supported: request receivables and payment-history data before proposing credit actions. Sales share is not outstanding credit exposure.

04 / Method & validation

The work behind the view.

One source of normalized records feeds every exhibit. The implementation rejects missing or duplicate headers, malformed rows, missing required fields, unmatched brands and staff, and invalid monetary types.

01 / Import

Map by header.

Read nine fictional source sheets with three different column orders. Locate sales and VAT by label, rather than column position.

02 / Normalize

Keep the grain clear.

Retain one record per sales line. Preserve document IDs so invoice counts do not inflate with the number of items.

03 / Analyze

Use aligned comparisons.

Compare Jan–Sep 2025 and 2026 sales, and track 2026 progress toward the full-year budget and target. Calculate invoice averages from matching totals and counts.

04 / Reconcile

Check every rollup.

Reconcile monthly, brand, customer group and staff totals, plus both margins of the allocation matrix.

Metric definitions & boundaries

Make the interpretation explicit.

  • Sales: sum of sales excluding VAT; no tax included.
  • Invoice: a distinct document ID, validated as one fictional invoice in this fixture.
  • FY attainment: Jan–Sep actual sales ÷ full-year budget or target.
  • YoY discrepancy: Jan–Sep 2026 sales − Jan–Sep 2025 sales; percent change divides by 2025 sales.
  • Customer group: an assigned fictional grouping; no real affiliation inference.

What this demonstration does not establish

No cost, collection or pipeline data is included. Profitability, credit exposure and a revenue forecast therefore remain outside the analysis.

The fixture contains positive sales only. A production workflow needs explicit handling of returns, adjustments, duplicate imports, mixed currencies and exceptions.

Outcome: a reproducible reporting demonstration with traceable metrics. Business actions are illustrative recommendations; no measured savings, management adoption or commercial impact is claimed.
